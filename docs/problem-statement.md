# Problem Statement

**The specific audience affected:**
Supply chain operations managers, logistics coordinators, and fleet operators globally.

**Why existing solutions don't solve it:**
Traditional supply chain management (SCM) software often relies on static scheduling and siloed data. When a disruption occurs (e.g., a port closure or extreme weather), operators must manually correlate the incident with their active shipments, routes, and available fleet. This manual process is slow, error-prone, and doesn't provide predictive risk scoring, meaning operators react to delays rather than proactively preventing them.

**Quantified pain:**
Supply chain disruptions can cost large enterprises millions of dollars annually in delayed goods, spoiled cold-chain cargo (such as vaccines or perishables), and expedited shipping fees. According to industry reports, reducing reaction time to disruptions by even 50% can save millions in logistics overhead.

**Why this problem matters now:**
Global supply chains are increasingly fragile, facing continuous threats from geopolitical tensions (e.g., Suez Canal blockages), extreme weather events due to climate change, and labor strikes. Real-time adaptability is no longer a luxury but a critical necessity for maintaining operations and fulfilling SLA commitments.
